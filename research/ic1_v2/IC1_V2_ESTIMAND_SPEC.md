# IC1-v2 estimand and acceptance specification

Version 2.0, 25 November 2026, Europe/Bucharest. This is the specification for an instrument calibration on known finite synthetic systems. It follows the historical defect replay and precedes v2 implementation and the acceptance replicates.

## Experiment and estimands

At a declared passive checkpoint, the finite microscopic state X includes sufficient history bookkeeping to make Z = φ(H) a deterministic cell label. H contains only observations and allowed actions before intervention. Each synthetic design supplies an analytically justified passive checkpoint law μ as exact nonnegative integer masses divided by their positive total. Conditioning within a cell gives ν_z(x) = μ(x) / μ(Z=z). This is a controlled simulator oracle with declared start labels and a known start design. Biological estimation of these inputs and arbitrary-chain stationary inference are outside this instrument.

After selecting X=x, apply the specified nonempty sequence of transition operators a = (a₁, …, aₕ). One element means one transition. A single intervention followed by passive evolution lists the intervention once and then passive kernels. A persistent intervention lists the same operator at every step through the declared horizon. The response Y is sampled from a fixed categorical emission law after transition h, independently given the terminal microstate. All compared start states and resolutions share this external outcome alphabet. A categorical cell transition can be recovered as a special case when the declared emission is that cell label.

Let Kₓᵃ be the terminal response law given x. The passive-conditioned intervention law is Pᵃ(Y | Z=z) = ∑ₓ ν_z(x) Kₓᵃ. The two scores below measure variation among its components.

- **Support worst case.** D_z is the maximum TV between Kₓᵃ and Kₓ′ᵃ over all pairs of starts in S_z = {x | μ(x)>0 and φ(H)=z}. The global diameter is max_z D_z across occupied cells.
- **Occupancy-weighted error.** W_z is the expected TV between Kₓᵃ and Kₓ′ᵃ for two independent starts drawn from ν_z. Draws with equal starts contribute zero and remain in the denominator. Global W = ∑_z μ(Z=z) W_z. This is a new unconditional pairwise expectation. Historical IC1 used distinct-pair normalization across cells for its separate mean-pairwise statistic.

TV is half the L1 distance on the common response alphabet. A positive-mass singleton has diameter and weighted error zero. A zero-mass cell is omitted and identified as unsupported through the declared start design. At least one start must have positive mass. Constant abstraction can hide variation when both the start cell and the response are collapsed. Keeping the external outcome fixed tests this degeneracy directly. These diameters describe within-design incompatibility. Identifiability across alternative mechanisms requires comparing their observable and interventional laws.

## Support and numerics

Determine admissible starts using exact positivity of their integer masses. Separate impossible states, rare positive states, and states outside passive support that can occur during or after intervention. Apply the passive support restriction **once**, to the starts. All legal transitions and terminal emissions are retained after that restriction. In the rare-start fixture, report both full positive support and the historical relative within-cell floor of 1e−6 as a labelled sensitivity with the exact excluded passive mass. A finite occupancy sample can miss a rare positive state. Record omitted positive starts while retaining them in the support estimator.

The oracle independently propagates each admissible point mass through the ordered kernels and terminal emission and enumerates pairs. The trajectory estimator uses the simulator, empirical response histograms and an independent aggregation. The two paths share fixture data, the external outcome definition and exact checkpoint design. Probabilities of finite kernel propagation and simulated categorical transitions use double precision. The integer-mass design avoids any numerical support threshold.

## Trajectory sampling and intervals

For the diameter, draw n independent complete trajectories per positive start and maximize empirical row TV within each cell. For the weighted error, independently draw m ordered start pairs per occupied cell from its passive mass law. Separately draw n complete response trajectories for each distinct selected start, and average the empirical row TVs over drawn pairs. Start draws use integer tickets. A common row estimate can be reused across pairs. Conditional sampling at the labelled checkpoint is equivalent to reset under the checkpoint design.

Let k be the response alphabet size and s the number of fitted rows. For n independent trajectories per row, the simultaneous radius `r = sqrt(log(2^(k+1) * s / alpha_r) / (2*n))` follows by applying two-sided Hoeffding to each of the 2^k response subsets and taking a union bound across subsets and rows. Pair TV error is at most 2r. For the diameter use alpha_r=0.05, apply the bound within every cell and propagate the intervals through the maximum. For the weighted estimate use alpha_r=0.025 conditional on the drawn starts, multiply 2r by the observed unequal-pair fraction in the cell, and add `sqrt(log(2*c/0.025)/(2*m))` for c occupied cells by Hoeffding on the independent true-TV pairs. Propagate through the passive cell weights. Clip to [0,1]. Singleton variation is exactly zero. These are conservative 95% simultaneous intervals separately for each sampling mode. The bounds apply to independent simulated trajectories with fixed finite alphabets and known checkpoint design.

## Prespecified calibration

- Synthetic cases and analytic global targets (D,W): ring L1 (0.70,0.35), ring L2 (0,0), ring L2/asym (0.85,0.425), historical four-state (1,0.5), rare variant (1,0.0000000199999998), structurally impossible (0,0), intervention-only endpoint (1,0.5), two-step exit (1,0.5), persistent two-step (0,0), coincident-support stochastic null (0,0), coincident-support multicategory alternative (0.5,0.25), constant cell with fixed response (1,0.5), and constant response control (0,0). The four-state and deterministic controls anchor exact edge cases.
- Run 256 independent synthetic replicates per case and grid point. Set n=m to 128, 512 and 8192. Master NumPy SeedSequence entropy is 25112026, using explicit child keys (fixture order, grid order, replicate, mode). Smoke tests use disjoint seeds.
- Require all analytic targets within absolute 1e−12, every deterministic diameter exact within that tolerance, and zero invalid or missing estimates. For each grid point, case and mode, require at least 0.92 observed simultaneous coverage across all occupied cells and the global scalar. Report binomial uncertainty for that rate. At 8192, globally require absolute bias at most 0.025, RMSE at most 0.03, and mean global interval width at most 0.12. In the ring L1, ring L2/asym, coincident-support stochastic null and multicategory alternative, require global RMSE at 8192 at most 0.40 times RMSE at 128 in each mode. These criteria evaluate capability for a synthetic TV tolerance of order 0.05.
- Record means, signed bias, RMSE, error quantiles, coverage with Wilson intervals, mean interval width and rare-state omissions. Run the same frozen grid twice and require deterministic acceptance JSON bytes with the pinned source and environment. Retain failed results if any gate fails. Keep execution local and bounded to two hours cumulative compute.
- Scientific gate also requires the independent oracle/simulation tests, support-shift and multi-step controls, canonical fingerprint tests, and provenance verification. No learned IC2 scores, held-out MSc outcomes or biological benchmarks enter this calibration.
