# Risk definitions and exact derivations

Let q(I) be any three-category forecast using input I, and Q_c(P,V) the checkpoint-specific response law under the single probe. For each c, integrate TV(q(I), Q_c(P,V)) over the six equiprobable (P,V) values **before** taking a mechanism maximum. Minimax is inf_q max_c R_c(q), whereas the balanced Bayes optimum is inf_q (R₀(q)+R₁(q))/2. A feasible action attaining the Bayes lower bound separately in each mechanism certifies the minimax optimum. The equal prior matters for the Bayes result. The minimax adversarial class contains exactly two fixed mechanisms and can choose a single c for the risk expectation. [Two-point argument context](https://web.stanford.edu/class/stats300a/REFS/yu1997assouad.pdf)

The passive kernel is independent of C and preserves independent uniform position and velocity. The history H contains the ordered positions (P−V mod 3, P); there are six equally probable histories conditional on either mechanism. The one-pass test enumerates their rational masses. Longer passive laws also agree by identical passive kernels and initialization. The intervention puts mass 17/20 at P+V+c and 3/20 at P−V+c, modulo three.

| Permitted input | Equal-prior Bayes optimum | Minimax optimum | Derivation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Full H, sensor masked | 17/40 | 17/40 | At **every** H the two response laws are TV 17/20 apart. The triangle inequality lower-bounds their balanced mean by 17/40. Their midpoint attains that loss in both mechanisms at every history. |
| Full H with S | 0 | 0 | Displacement determines V, and S provides c. The conditional action equals the exact row at every history/sensor pair. |
| Compressed Z with S | 7/20 | 7/20 | For fixed P and c, velocities are equiprobable. The two laws exchange 17/20 and 3/20 on two different positions, so their TV is 7/10. The midpoint attains 7/20 at each start in each mechanism. |
| Compressed Z, sensor masked | 17/40 | 17/40 | At P=0 the four rows are rotations of a common panel as P varies. The coordinate median intervals there are [0,3/20], [0,3/20], [3/20,17/20]. The vector (3/40, 3/40, 17/20) minimizes expected L1 subject to summing to one. It attains 17/40 after averaging the two velocities in each mechanism. Rotate the action with P. |

For the last cell, the four rows at P=0 are (0,17/20,3/20), (0,3/20,17/20), (3/20,0,17/20), (17/20,0,3/20). Its **conditional mean law** is (1/4,1/4,1/2), with Bayes TV risk 19/40. Thus the infinite-data target of the ordinary categorical table costs an additional **1/20 TV** there. This objective gap is zero in the other three conditions. The rational oracle independently minimizes a separable piecewise-linear L1 loss on the simplex and verifies the feasible balanced minimax witness. It also cross-checks all probe rows against finite kernel propagation. [Rational oracle](../../src/neurobranch/abstraction/ic1_msc_oracle.py)

## Calibrated decomposition

Within a fixed arm and for the same evaluation law, let R_H* be the full-input optimum, R_Z* the compressed-input optimum, R_F,Z* the optimum in the table class, and R_fit the risk of the fitted table. All finite categorical maps are representable in this class, so R_F,Z*−R_Z*=0. Smoothing is part of the fitting rule. The information gap R_Z*−R_H* is exactly 0 for base and 7/20 for sensor. The total learned excess R_fit−R_H* decomposes into information gap, model-class approximation gap and fitted excess R_fit−R_F,Z*. Report the latter as the sum of the fixed conditional-mean objective gap and the finite-fit deviation R_fit−R_mean. That deviation can be negative. There is no iterative optimization. The fitter's contrast across compression conditions also includes the difference in these fitted excess terms. Apply this decomposition to the exact population risks of the fitted tables as a secondary analysis; the primary paired episode estimate carries evaluation sampling noise.

IC1-v2's support diameter D and occupancy-weighted pair TV W describe within-cell response-law variation before selecting a prediction rule. The numerical equality between W and 17/40 on one symmetric two-state fixture is specific to that design. The learned benchmark's Bayes and minimax risks use one prediction and one reference law.
